Who paid the 2025–26 US tariffs? Using Census import data for every product and country, pre-duty import prices barely moved when tariffs rose. Of each $1 of duty, roughly 96–100¢ was added to what US importers paid. Same as 2018–19.

https://johnpospisil.github.io/tariff-pass-through/

**2/**
Method: the Amiti–Redding–Weinstein design. 12-month change in unit values for 3.7m product × country × month observations, regressed on the tariff change, comparing varieties of the same product in the same month. 0 = buyers pay the duty, −1 = exporters absorb it.

**3/**
Estimate: +0.04 (SE 0.03), or −0.04 with outliers trimmed. BLS import price indexes by country of origin agree (+0.01). The one exception is Section 232 metals and autos, where exporters cut prices by about 9% of the duty.

**4/**
Duties collected ran well below the rates on paper: a 10.8% effective rate at the October 2025 peak against 16.8% statutory, because of USMCA claims, in-transit exemptions and duties on metal content only.

**5/**
In stores, CPI prices of exposed goods rose by roughly 0.3–0.65 of the duty cost of their imports, measured against each item's own trend. These estimates are imprecise and none is significant at 5%.

**6/**
Trade shifted: China's share of US imports of the goods tariffed in 2018–19 fell from 25.5% in 2017 to 7.4% in the year to August 2026. Taiwan, Vietnam, Mexico and Southeast Asia gained. Importers also stockpiled about $71bn above trend before the 2025 actions.
